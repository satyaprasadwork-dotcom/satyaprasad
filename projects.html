Independent publishing · Technology

Ninth Post: making technical stories navigable

Visit Ninth Post

Who it serves

Ninth Post is an editorial website covering AI, models, emerging technology, robots, policy and product thinking. Visitors need a fast way to find a topic, assess whether an article will answer a specific question and follow related reporting.

Visible product choices

The homepage groups articles by subject and gives prominence to recent stories. Article cards include summaries and reading-time context; editorial pages describe the site's approach. Those choices work together: categorization helps discovery, descriptive summaries set expectations and an accessible editorial policy helps readers understand how material is selected and checked.

The underlying product trade-off

Adding articles increases coverage but can also create navigational clutter, duplicated subjects and thin category pages. A publishing workflow therefore needs more than a template: new entries should land in the right category, link to useful related work and retain specific evidence. Technical SEO helps discovery, but it cannot substitute for an article that answers the reader's question.

What I would measure

Useful measures include indexed canonical URLs, broken-link rate, article engagement in context, mobile performance and reader navigation between related topics. No private traffic or revenue figures are asserted here.

Community publishing · Anime

Indian Anime Club: design for a specific audience

Visit Indian Anime Club

The audience decision

The site speaks directly to anime fans in India, rather than treating all fandoms as identical. Familiar cultural touchpoints, genre choices and community-focused copy create a specific point of view while keeping the experience welcoming to new fans.

Visible experience

The homepage introduces the community before moving into genres, character exploration, recommendations and articles. Its city chapter section explicitly says that local chapters are not running yet. That distinction matters: a community product should not suggest an activity is available when it is only planned.

Why the editorial structure matters

Community sites have two complementary jobs. One is helping someone discover an interesting story. The other is helping them feel safe and able to participate. Article navigation, contact information, community guidelines and a clear boundary between current features and future plans support those jobs.

Practical lessons

Audience-specific language can make a site feel distinctive, but it has to remain understandable and usable across devices. A playful visual element should not obscure the next action. Editorial consistency and straightforward navigation become more important as the number of articles grows.
