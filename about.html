About my work

I work at the point where a problem, a product decision and a delivery team meet. This site brings that professional path together with the things I write and build independently.

How I approach a problem

Good product work starts before the solution. I want to know who is facing a problem, what they are trying to accomplish, what breaks in their current process and what evidence would tell us that a change helped. That means translating conversations into testable requirements instead of beginning with a feature list.

My background spans software development, research-oriented moderation work, digital marketing, business analysis and product management. Those experiences make me attentive to the details that often fall between disciplines: language in a requirement, edge cases in a workflow, the cost of a manual exception and the handoff between a client and a developer.

A practical working method

  1. Frame the decision. Write the user's objective and the outcome the product needs to achieve. Separate what is observed from what is assumed.
  2. Make the workflow visible. Sketch the current steps, roles, inputs, decisions and failure points before proposing a new screen.
  3. Scope the smallest useful release. Define boundaries, dependencies and acceptance criteria, then prioritize the risky uncertainties.
  4. Check the result. Use task-level outcomes, qualitative feedback and defects to refine the next version rather than treating shipment as success.
